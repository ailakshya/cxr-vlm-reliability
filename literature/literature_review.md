# Check every claim against the image

**Yes, you can write this paper, and it is a good topic for late 2026, as long as the contribution is framed narrowly.** As of 29 October 2026, the research notes found no published work that does all three of the following for chest X-ray (CXR) vision-language models (VLMs): (i) check each generated finding claim against independent image evidence, such as expert bounding boxes; (ii) apply controlled shifts across sites, image corruptions, acquisition conditions and demographics; and (iii) evaluate calibration or abstention under those shifts. The five closest papers each cover at most two of the three. They are the predictive-entropy MIMIC→PadChest study ([arXiv 2604.08941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08941)), HalluCXR ([arXiv 2605.20469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20469)), MVC-Bench ([arXiv 2608.27004](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27004)), MAIRA-2/RadFact ([arXiv 2406.04449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04449)) and DualRead/CCG-AUC ([arXiv 2609.06419](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06419)). The field is moving fast, though. Roughly one adjacent preprint has appeared each month of 2026, so a generic "calibration of medical VLMs under shift" benchmark is already crowded and carries **moderate-to-high scoop risk**. The defensible, lower-risk version is this: a claim-level **evidence-support rate**, computed against expert boxes from several sites, used both as a reference-free faithfulness metric and as an abstention signal, and tested on whether it stays calibrated when the site or image quality changes. You can start this study **today on open or free-registration data**: IU-Xray, NIH ChestX-ray14 plus ChestX-Det, CheXpert Plus, Shenzhen/Montgomery and synthetic corruptions. The models fit on your RTX 5070 Ti, which has 16 GB of VRAM (my figure, not from the notes), at 4–8B scale with quantisation. PhysioNet sets (MIMIC-CXR, MS-CXR, VinDr-CXR) and PadChest-GR then add the in-distribution anchor and the strongest grounding sites as access arrives. The realistic target is **MICCAI 2027 (deadline likely late February 2027)**, with the MICCAI UNSURE workshop or a Springer/IEEE journal as fallbacks. MIDL 2027 (around early December 2026) is too close.

A note on verification before the details. The notes use three markers, which this report keeps. **[K]** means an ID or fact taken from the researchers' prior knowledge and not re-fetched. **UNVERIFIED** means it could not be confirmed. Unmarked items with links were confirmed against arXiv, Hugging Face (HF) or PhysioNet pages during the research session. GPU memory figures marked "≈" are rule-of-thumb inferences (bf16 ≈ 2 bytes per parameter plus overhead), not vendor numbers. Two further cautions: the notes' search of 2026 preprints was about 15 queries deep, and MICCAI 2026 and workshop papers were not fully indexed.

## Grounded CXR VLMs now exist with open weights, but only five emit boxes natively

The 2024–2026 model landscape divides cleanly into models that output localised evidence and models that output only text. **MAIRA-2** (Microsoft, 7B) introduced *grounded report generation*. It emits a list of (sentence, boxes) pairs, built from a frozen RAD-DINO encoder and a fully fine-tuned Vicuna-7B. It was trained on MIMIC-CXR, PadChest, a private US mix and MS-CXR, and it is gated on HF under a research-only licence ([arXiv 2406.04449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04449); [HF card](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/maira-2)). **MedGemma 1.5 4B** (Google, April 2026) added bounding-box anatomical localisation and longitudinal CXR. Its abstract reports a "35% increase in IoU on chest X-rays" but names neither the dataset nor whether the gain is relative or absolute ([arXiv 2604.05081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05081)). A third-party blog puts it at ≈3% → ≈38% IoU, which should be checked against the report's tables ([tinyweights](https://tinyweights.dev/posts/medgemma-1-5/)). **RadVLM** (ETH Zurich) handles report generation, classification, box grounding and dialogue, with weights only through PhysioNet credentialed access ([arXiv 2502.03333](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03333v1); [PhysioNet](https://physionet.org/content/radvlm-model)). **AnatomiX** (January 2026) detects anatomy first and then grounds phrases through an LLM, and releases code and weights ([arXiv 2601.03191](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03191)). The general-domain **Qwen2.5-VL** outputs native JSON boxes under Apache-2.0 ([arXiv 2502.13923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13923), [K]).

Most other CXR models produce text only. CheXagent-2-3B is MIT-licensed and not gated ([HF](https://huggingface.co/StanfordAIMI/CheXagent-2-3b)). Lingshu-7B is MIT-licensed, uses the Qwen2.5-VL architecture, and its medical grounding has not been evaluated ([HF](https://huggingface.co/lingshu-medical-mllm/Lingshu-7B)). LLaVA-Rad ([arXiv 2403.08002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08002), [K]), LLaVA-Med ([arXiv 2306.00890](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00890), [K]), RadFM ([arXiv 2308.02463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.02463), [K]), XrayGPT, RaDialog and Med-Flamingo (all [K]) are likewise text-only. These need post-hoc evidence extraction, either Grad-CAM or attention pointing, or prompting the model for coordinates. That measures something different from native grounding, and the paper must keep the two apart. Contrastive models (BiomedCLIP [arXiv 2303.00915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00915), CheXzero [DOI 10.1038/s41551-022-00936-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41551-022-00936-9), both [K]) give only similarity heatmaps. They are still useful as cheap baselines.

## Existing metrics score reports against other reports, not against the image

The central weakness this paper attacks is easy to state. **Nearly every CXR report metric compares the generated report to a reference report.** That holds for CheXbert F1 ([arXiv 2004.09167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09167)), RadGraph F1 ([arXiv 2106.14463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14463)), GREEN ([arXiv 2405.03595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03595)), RaTEScore ([arXiv 2406.16845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16845)), FineRadScore ([arXiv 2405.20613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20613)), CheXprompt ([arXiv 2403.08002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08002)) and RadCliQ (Patterns 2023; DOI [10.1016/j.patter.2023.100802](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100802) is **UNVERIFIED**). RadFact, from MAIRA-2, adds a grounding component: a per-sentence spatial check of predicted boxes against reference boxes ([arXiv 2406.04449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04449)). A snippet puts MAIRA-2 at roughly 68 grounding precision and 92 grounding recall; check this against the paper's tables before quoting it ([MS Research PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prodnew/2024/06/2406.04449v1.pdf)). FactCheXcker re-verifies against the image, but only for measurements such as tube position ([arXiv 2411.18672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18672)). ReXTrust detects hallucinations reference-free from hidden states, with AUROC 0.875, but it is a model-internal signal and not an image-evidence check ([arXiv 2412.15264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15264)).

This matters most under shift. Reference-based metrics break when reporting style changes across sites. MAIRA-2 on PadChest scored ROUGE 28% and BLEU-4 10% but logical precision/recall of 57%/49%, and the authors blame the low lexical scores partly on PadChest lacking indication, technique and comparison sections ([arXiv 2406.04449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04449)). Moving to an out-of-distribution (OOD) HOPPR set cut BLEU, ROUGE-L and BERTScore by about 3–15 points ([arXiv 2505.24223](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24223)). "Reporting Practice Matters" shows that the choice of reference report alone moves the scores ([arXiv 2609.19093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19093)). Med-Gemini skipped RadGraph F1 on its Indian IND1 set because RadGraph is tuned to MIMIC-style reports ([arXiv 2405.03162](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03162)). The paper's sharpest argument follows from this. **When you move a VLM to a new hospital, existing metrics cannot separate "the model got worse" from "the reports are written differently."** A box-based evidence check sidesteps reference style altogether.

Grounding itself is scored with box IoU. The usual measures are mIoU and Acc@IoU>0.5 under the MedRPG protocol on MS-CXR ([arXiv 2507.02994](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.02994)). Classic grounding baselines stay below 45% mIoU ([arXiv 2604.01915](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.01915)), and anatomy-pretrained models reach 61.2 ([arXiv 2502.16585](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16585v1)). Saliency methods add the pointing game and contrast-to-noise ratio (CNR) ([K]). Two traps make published numbers incomparable. MS-CXR splits differ across papers (890 vs 1,153 samples) ([arXiv 2502.16585](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16585v1)). And MAIRA-2 trained on about half of MS-CXR, so its ≈57.8 mIoU there is contaminated (secondary source: [emergentmind](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/maira-2)).

Three findings from these studies anchor your motivation. In the npj Digital Medicine artefact study, weak artefacts cut CXR accuracy by **10.46%** on average, and models flagged strong artefacts only **11.5%** of the time. MedGemma's reasoning–answer conflict rate doubled from 0.204 to 0.427 under weak motion ([PMC12660864](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12660864/)). VLMs, in other words, keep answering confidently on images they can no longer read properly. HalluCXR found hallucinations in **61.9–82.3%** of outputs from six VLMs on MIMIC, with response length predicting hallucination at AUC up to 0.908 ([arXiv 2605.20469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20469)). Flamingo-CXR showed that the same system degrades on ICU/portable films, where clinicians preferred its reports in 56.1% of cases against 77.7% for in/outpatient films. That is an acquisition shift inside one hospital ([DOI 10.1038/s41591-024-03302-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-03302-1)).

On uncertainty, the toolbox is ready to borrow. Semantic-consistency sampling for report generation (RRG) showed that abstaining on the 20% most uncertain reports raises factuality by 10% ([arXiv 2412.04606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04606)). Conformal Factuality filters claims with guarantees ([arXiv 2402.10978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10978)). Conformal Alignment uses RRG as a main use case ([arXiv 2405.10301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10301)). SCoRE extends selective conformal risk control to distribution shift and includes a radiology-report application ([arXiv 2603.24704](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24704)). CONRep applies conformal prediction to VLM impression drafting on ChestX-Det10 and Open-I, in a Springer journal ([DOI 10.1007/s10278-026-02179-5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10278-026-02179-5)). "Adapt or Calibrate?" shows that certified coverage in medical VQA is tiny (11.5% on SLAKE at 20% error), and that 500 local labels raise it from 6.8% to 37.6% ([arXiv 2606.15910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15910)). That result bears directly on whether calibration from one site transfers to another.

### The five closest papers and what each lacks

The nearest neighbour is **2604.08941** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08941)). It tests MedGemma-4B and LLaVA-Rad on MIMIC (n=196) and PadChest (n=861) with binary presence questions, and shows that predictive entropy supports selective prediction across sites: 88.5% answered at a 5% risk target. It has no grounding, no free-text reports, one shift pair and a small n. **HalluCXR** has the hallucination taxonomy but only MIMIC data and no localisation. **MVC-Bench** covers calibration under domain shift for CXR, but for CLIP-style classifiers rather than generative VLMs. **MAIRA-2/RadFact** has grounded reports and a grounding metric, but RadFact depends on references and the paper has no calibration or shift analysis. **DualRead/CCG-AUC** comes closest to "evidence-grounded confidence": it tests whether confidence drops when image substitution makes the model wrong. But that evidence is counterfactual image swapping, not region-level evidence, and the task is VQA rather than CXR reports ([arXiv 2609.06419](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06419)).

What remains open is their intersection. Each positive finding claim in a generated report would be checked against expert boxes, giving an evidence-support rate. That rate would be reported per site, compared with reference-based metrics to show how those metrics are confounded by reporting style, and used as an abstention signal against entropy, semantic consistency and verbalised confidence. Two further questions follow. Do conformal claim filters calibrated on one site keep their guarantees on another? Do VLMs keep "seeing" findings whose evidence has been corrupted away? The notes searched specifically for this combination and found nothing ([distribution-shift notes Q4 citing 2604.10188, 2505.24223, 2411.15122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10188)). That is still an absence of evidence, so run a targeted arXiv and MICCAI 2026 search before committing.

## Enough good data is open to start today; credentialing adds the anchor

Good public data does exist. The catch is that **the open datasets with boxes are almost all NIH ChestX-ray14 derivatives**, and the open datasets with reports carry no boxes. An open-only study therefore has rich *grounding* evidence from a single US source, plus *report* and *label* data from three or four more. That is enough for a credible first paper. Credentialed and on-request sets turn it into a genuinely multi-site grounding study.

All "time to obtain" figures are my estimates; the notes record none. PhysioNet credentialing means an online CITI "Data or Specimens Only Research" course, which is a few hours of work, followed by an approval review and a DUA per dataset ([MS-CXR page](https://physionet.org/content/ms-cxr/)). Apply this week and plan for anything from a few days to several weeks. Note also that VinDr-PCXR is a *paediatric* set and not the same as VinDr-CXR ([PhysioNet](https://www.physionet.org/content/vindr-pcxr/)).

**The minimal open-only combination that runs the whole study is as follows.** ChestX-Det (plus NIH ChestX-ray14's ≈1,000 boxes and, optionally, RSNA opacity boxes) supplies the expert grounding evidence. IU-Xray and CheXpert Plus supply sites with reports, so you can measure how reference-based metrics move against the evidence-support metric. Shenzhen/Montgomery (China/US), the Mendeley KGMU/UPUMS/GMC-Kota set and NITRD Dataset A/B (India) give label-level sites from other countries. MedMNIST-C-style corruptions at five severities ([arXiv 2406.17536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17536)), applied to every set, give the controlled-severity shift axis. With models from HF (CheXagent-2, Qwen2.5-VL, Lingshu, and MedGemma 1.5 and MAIRA-2 after clicking through their gates), none of this waits on PhysioNet.

The honest limitation is that every model in the study was trained mainly on MIMIC. Without MIMIC you cannot show a true in-distribution baseline, so the open-only study measures *variation across sites and severities* rather than an ID-to-OOD drop. And all the open box data come from one source, NIH. **Credentialed sets add three things.** MIMIC-CXR and MS-CXR provide the in-distribution anchor, which is also where contamination becomes visible: MAIRA-2 and RadVLM trained on MS-CXR. VinDr-CXR provides a second, non-US grounding site with five-reader consensus test boxes, if those are released (UNVERIFIED). PadChest-GR, available on request, provides the only multi-reader grounded-*report* set outside MIMIC, which enables RadFact-style text-and-box checks in two languages. With those added, the paper can claim a three-continent grounding evaluation (US, Spain, Vietnam) instead of a single-source one.

## A feasible design: evidence-support rate as metric and abstention signal

**Working title:** "Does the evidence travel? Claim-level image-grounded reliability of chest X-ray VLMs under site and quality shift."

**Models (six, all ≤8B, one 16 GB GPU).** Use MedGemma 1.5 4B in bf16, MAIRA-2 7B in 8-bit, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B in 4-bit/8-bit as the box-native group. Use CheXagent-2-3B in bf16 and Lingshu-7B or LLaVA-Rad in 4-bit as the text-only group, with Grad-CAM or attention pointing and coordinate prompting. Add BiomedCLIP as a contrastive heatmap baseline. AnatomiX goes in if its size fits, and RadVLM once credentialing clears. That gives three evidence modes: native boxes, prompted coordinates and post-hoc saliency. Six models is enough for a pattern and few enough to finish.

**Claim extraction.** Generate a findings section for each image. Run a local CheXbert labeler ([arXiv 2004.09167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09167)) to extract positive finding claims, then map them to a shared label space of roughly the eight MS-CXR findings plus pneumothorax and nodule/mass. ChestX-Det, VinDr-CXR and PadChest-GR label sets all need hand-built mappings, and those mappings belong in an appendix. Manually check about 200 claims for extraction errors and report the error rate. This is cheap and reviewers will ask for it.

**Metrics.** The headline metric is **Evidence-Support Precision (ESP)**. Of the positive claims a model makes, what fraction refer to a finding for which expert evidence exists in that image? Where the model localises a claim (natively, by prompted box, or by saliency peak), it also requires spatial overlap: IoU ≥ 0.3 or a pointing-game hit. Its complements are the **unsupported-claim rate**, an evidence-defined hallucination rate, and **evidence recall**, the fraction of expert-boxed findings that the model mentions. Alongside these, report standard grounding (mIoU, Acc@IoU 0.3/0.5, pointing game). Where references exist, report reference-based scores (CheXbert F1, RadGraph F1, GREEN on IU-Xray, CheXpert Plus and MIMIC) so you can show directly how reference metrics and ESP diverge across sites. On corrupted images, add an **artefact-awareness rate**: does the report mention degraded quality? This follows the 11.5% detection result ([PMC12660864](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12660864/)).

**Shift axes.** Use four axes. *Site*: NIH/ChestX-Det, Indiana, Stanford and Shenzhen/Montgomery from open data, plus Indian hospitals (Mendeley KGMU/UPUMS/Kota, NITRD, later IN-CXR) as a label-level probe only, then MIMIC, Spain and Vietnam after access arrives. *Quality*: five corruption families (noise, blur, JPEG, contrast/gamma, rotation/crop) at three to five severities, after MedMNIST-C. *Acquisition*: AP vs PA and portable vs fixed, from view metadata in CheXpert Plus and MIMIC. *Demographic*: sex and age bins in CheXpert Plus and MIMIC, which is where underdiagnosis was established ([DOI 10.1038/s41591-021-01595-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-021-01595-0), [K]).

**Reliability and abstention.** For each claim, compute four confidence signals: token log-probability; semantic consistency over K≈5 samples ([arXiv 2412.04606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04606)); verbalised confidence; and a new **evidence-consistency score**, meaning agreement of the claim's box across samples, or saliency stability. Report ECE and Brier for claim correctness defined by ESP, AURC, and selective risk at 80% and 90% coverage, all per site and per severity. Then run a conformal claim filter ([arXiv 2402.10978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10978)) calibrated on one site, for example ChestX-Det/NIH, and measure realised coverage on the others. If the guarantee breaks, that is a headline result, and it ties to SCoRE's shift extension ([arXiv 2603.24704](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24704)) and to the local-label result ([arXiv 2606.15910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15910)).

**Hypotheses the paper tests.** H1: reference-based metric drops across sites overstate or misorder the drops in ESP, because reporting style changes. H2: under corruption, the unsupported-claim rate rises faster than models flag artefacts, so models keep describing evidence they can no longer see. H3: evidence-consistency beats entropy as an abstention signal OOD, even if not in-distribution. H4: conformal claim filters calibrated on one site under-cover on others. Each hypothesis is a figure, and a negative result on any of them is still publishable.

**Compute budget (my estimate).** Sample about 1,000 images per site. Six models × (1 greedy + 5 samples) × 6–7 sites gives about 250k generations; at about 3 s each for a 4–7B model on a 16 GB card, that is roughly 9–10 GPU-days. The corruption grid adds about 500 images × 5 families × 3 severities per model, around another 4–5 days. The whole study fits in about three weeks of wall-clock GPU time. Cut K to 3 samples if needed.

**Expected contributions.** First, an image-evidence faithfulness metric (ESP and the unsupported-claim rate) that does not depend on reference reports, validated against expert boxes from up to four sources. Second, the first multi-site, multi-severity measurement of grounding and faithfulness for CXR report-generating VLMs. Third, a comparison of evidence-based and uncertainty-based abstention under shift, including whether conformal guarantees transfer. Fourth, released code and label mappings.

## Conclusion

The opening is real but narrow. It exists because the community has built hallucination counters, calibration methods and shift benchmarks around *reference text and VQA answers*, while the one independent signal that survives a change of hospital, an expert box on the image, has been used only to score grounding in-distribution. Recasting expert boxes as a claim-level faithfulness metric and abstention signal is a small conceptual step, but nobody has taken it across sites. That makes it a good M.Tech paper: inference-only, single-GPU, startable on open data this week, and useful whether H1–H4 hold or fail. The main risk is speed, not feasibility. Given the monthly pace of adjacent 2026 preprints, the decisive actions are to run a targeted arXiv/MICCAI 2026 scoop check in the first week, to keep the claim centred on image evidence rather than generic calibration, and to put a preprint on arXiv before MICCAI 2027.

**Recommendation: go.** Build the open-data pipeline now, apply for PhysioNet and PadChest-GR in parallel, and aim for MICCAI 2027, with UNSURE or a Springer/IEEE journal as the fallback.
